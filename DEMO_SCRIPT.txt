────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  [0:20 – 0:55]  NODE 1 — FOREST ACOUSTIC NODE (NODE_01)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  SHOW: Pick up the forest node. Close-up on INMP441 mic module and Ra-01H.

  SAY:
  "Node one is the Forest Acoustic Node — the core of this system.
  It captures five-second audio windows from this INMP441 I2S microphone
  and streams raw PCM audio over USB serial at 921,600 baud to the
  companion AI pipeline running on the host machine."

  "That pipeline runs a YAMNet transfer learning model we fine-tuned on
  the ESC-50 and FSC-22 datasets — seven threat classes including chainsaw,
  gunshot, and vehicle — achieving 87.16% test accuracy on held-out data.
  This is not a pre-trained off-the-shelf classifier. It is our model."

  "When a threat class exceeds 70% confidence across two consecutive
  five-second windows, the result is transmitted over LoRa to the gateway."

  SHOW: Point to Ra-01H module.

  SAY:
  "The LoRa radio is the Ai-Thinker Ra-01H — SX1276 chip,
  866 megahertz, India ISM band, sync word 0x34."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  [0:55 – 1:25]  NODE 2 — FOREST FIRE AND SMOKE MODULE (FOR01)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  SHOW: Close-up on MQ-2 sensor board and DHT22 attached to the forest node.
        Then show the 10k+22k voltage divider resistors.

  SAY:
  "We extended the forest node with a fire and smoke detection module.
  This is an MQ-2 semiconductor gas sensor for smoke, and a DHT22
  for ambient temperature and humidity. Both share the same ESP32
  as the acoustic pipeline — they use the same LoRa radio but transmit
  independently on their own five-second cycle."

  "The risk score combines smoke deviation from a rolling 20-sample baseline,
  temperature above ambient, and falling humidity. Three corroborating signals.
  When all three elevate together, confidence rises. A single signal alone
  produces a lower score — this reduces false positives from isolated events
  like cooking smoke or a sudden temperature spike."

  SHOW: Point to resistor divider.

  SAY:
  "The MQ-2 analog output swings toward five volts under high gas
  concentration. This 10-kilohm and 22-kilohm voltage divider steps it
  down to 3.3 volts before the ESP32 ADC on GPIO 34 — protecting the pin.
  The DHT22 data line sits on GPIO 4, confirmed free of the I2S mic wiring."

  "We state this clearly in our documentation: MQ-2 requires 24 to 48 hours
  of burn-in before calibration. The thresholds in this firmware are
  validated MVP defaults, not field-calibrated values. That is Phase 2."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  [1:25 – 1:55]  NODE 3 — URBAN / INDUSTRIAL NODE (URB01)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  SHOW: Pick up the urban node. Close-up on MQ-135. Show voltage divider.

  SAY:
  "Node three is the Urban and Industrial Hazard Node. The MQ-135 sensor
  detects air quality anomalies — carbon dioxide, ammonia, benzene, and
  related pollutants. Rather than reporting absolute ppm values — which
  requires reference-gas calibration — this node measures deviation from
  a 20-sample rolling baseline. 15% above baseline triggers a Watch.
  80% above baseline triggers Critical."

  "This baseline-relative approach makes the sensor self-calibrating to its
  local ambient environment, whether deployed in a factory district or
  a forest boundary."

  SHOW: Point to MQ-135 VCC wire going to VIN.

  SAY:
  "The MQ-135 heater runs on 5 volts from the VIN pin — not 3.3 volts.
  Running these semiconductor gas sensors at 3.3 volts gives unreliable
  readings. This is a common wiring error we document explicitly."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  [1:55 – 2:25]  NODE 4 — RIVERINE NODE (RIV01)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  SHOW: Pick up riverine node. Close-up on HC-SR04 sensor.

  SAY:
  "Node four is the Riverine Flash-Flood Risk Node. The HC-SR04 ultrasonic
  sensor measures the distance to the water surface. Subtract from the
  sensor's mounting height, and you have water level in centimetres.
  A six-sample, 30-second rolling history gives you rate-of-rise
  in centimetres per minute."

  "The risk score combines absolute water level and rate-of-rise.
  A slow rise to moderate depth is a Watch.
  A rapid rise — even from a low starting level — can push directly to
  Critical, because flash flood risk is determined more by rise rate
  than by absolute depth at any single moment."

  SHOW: Point to Echo pin wiring and divider.

  SAY:
  "The HC-SR04 Echo output is a five-volt signal.
  This voltage divider on GPIO 33 is non-negotiable — 5 volts directly
  into an ESP32 GPIO will damage it. The Trig pin on GPIO 25 connects
  direct — 3.3 volts is a valid trigger level for the HC-SR04."

  "The mounting height in firmware defaults to 200 centimetres.
  Before any real deployment this must be physically measured on-site
  and updated in the firmware constant."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  [2:25 – 2:55]  GATEWAY NODE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  SHOW: Gateway ESP32 connected to the laptop via USB.
        Serial monitor visible with packets scrolling.

  SAY:
  "The fourth ESP32 is the Gateway. No sensors. Its sole job is to listen
  on 866 megahertz, validate incoming VCN1-protocol packets from all three
  sender nodes, append RSSI and SNR signal quality metadata, and forward
  one line per packet over USB serial to the host machine at 115,200 baud."

  SHOW: Point to serial monitor output.

  SAY:
  "You can see packets arriving from URB01, RIV01, and FOR01 in real time.
  Each line is a comma-separated VCN1 packet — node ID, hazard type,
  risk score zero to one hundred, severity tier, confidence, sensor-specific
  values, and a firmware millisecond timestamp.
  The gateway validates the VCN1 prefix before forwarding.
  Malformed packets are dropped and logged."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  [2:55 – 3:00]  TRANSITION SHOT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  SHOW: Wide shot of all four nodes together.

  SAY:
  "Four nodes. Three hazard environments. One gateway.
  Let us now look at the software layer that ingests this data,
  fuses it, and surfaces actionable alerts."
